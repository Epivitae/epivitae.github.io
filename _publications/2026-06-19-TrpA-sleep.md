## Abstract
Tryptamine, a metabolic intermediate of tryptophan, accumulates in cerebrospinal fluid during wakefulness and dissipates during sleep, serving as an endogenous sleep-pressure signal. Here, we show that tryptamine is released in an activity-dependent manner by wake-active monoaminergic neurons in the locus coeruleus, dorsal raphe, and ventral tegmental area. Using a genetically encoded fluorescent sensor for tryptamine, we demonstrate that tryptamine directly binds and activates the orphan receptor GPR139 on inhibitory neurons in the hypothalamic preoptic area (POA), enhancing their excitability via Gq/11-mediated inhibition of GIRK potassium channels to promote sleep. Knockout of GPR139 in the POA disrupts sleep architecture and abolishes compensatory sleep rebound after sleep deprivation. Furthermore, small-molecule GPR139 agonists, including JNJ-63533054 and TAK-041, significantly extend sleep duration and deepen sleep depth. Human genetic analyses reveal that polymorphisms in *GPR139* and *AADC* are associated with increased risk of sleep disorders. Together, these findings establish the tryptamine–GPR139 signaling pathway as a critical molecular link between waking activity and sleep need, enriching the classical flip-flop model of sleep regulation and identifying a promising therapeutic target for insomnia.
